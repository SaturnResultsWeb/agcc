## Users

Four audiences arrive at the home page, each with a different job:

- **Operators and applicants.** Gambling businesses weighing Alderney as a jurisdiction, or already in the application process. They need the regulatory framework, licence and certificate types, fees, forms, ICS guidelines and a clear sense of how to start.
- **Players and the public.** People checking whether a gambling site is genuinely licensed, setting a limitation on play, or making a complaint. Often anxious or frustrated, often on a phone. They need the licence register and the complaints route without hunting.
- **Press, government and other regulators.** Reading news, notices, reports and leadership announcements, and judging the Commission's standing internationally.
- **Compliance professionals.** Lawyers, auditors and MLROs who return often for legislation, AML/CFT resources, sanctions lists and the register. They value speed and precision over storytelling.

## Product Purpose

The AGCC regulates electronic gambling licensed in Alderney, Channel Islands. Its mission: to ensure and maintain the integrity of electronic gambling activities regulated by the Commission.

The new home page must, in this order:

1. **Establish authority.** Make Alderney read as one of the most credible, modern eGambling jurisdictions in the world, so operators want to be licensed here and the public trusts the licence.
2. **Route every audience in one step.** Licence register search, apply, complain, read the law, latest news: each reachable directly from the home page.

Success for the demo: the Commission sees a site that feels like a serious international regulator, clearly better than their current Wix site, and commissions the full rebuild. Success in production: fewer "is this site licensed?" and "how do I complain?" enquiries, and operators reaching the right guidance without phoning.

This is a demo with full creative freedom: the palette, typography and even the logo may be rethought as a rebrand concept.

## Brand Personality

**Modern, transparent, firm.**

- **Modern:** a regulator that is current with the industry it oversees. Digital-native, fast, precise. Not heritage cosplay.
- **Transparent:** publishes openly. The register, decisions, notices and fees are front and centre, not buried. Plain English over legalese where possible.
- **Firm:** enforces without drama. Calm, measured tone; confident restraint rather than salesmanship. Never pleading, never flashy.

Emotional goals: operators feel "this is a serious jurisdiction I want to be part of"; players feel "I can check this and I will be heard"; press feel "this body is credible and in control".

Voice: short declarative sentences, concrete nouns, real numbers, no marketing filler.

## Reference Lanes

Study the DNA of these (blend, do not copy):

- **Central-bank editorial** (Bank of England, Sveriges Riksbank, De Nederlandsche Bank): calm typographic authority, data presented with literacy, institutional confidence without ornament.
- **Modern government design** (GOV.UK, Estonia e-Residency, Monetary Authority of Singapore): task-first routing, ruthless clarity, search as a first-class tool.
- **Premium jurisdiction marketing** (Jersey Finance, Guernsey Finance, Monaco): place-led identity, polished pitch to international business, the island as an asset.

## Anti-references

- **Casino and gambling glitz:** no chips, cards, dice, neon, gold, dark Vegas mood or slot-machine energy. The regulator is the opposite of the product it regulates.
- **SaaS startup template:** no gradient blobs, no bento of icon cards, no hero stat bar, no "trusted by" marquee.
- **Dusty council website:** no grey boxes, carousels, clip-art, walls of link soup.
- **Navy-and-gold bank:** no category-default corporate finance palette (the generated MASTER.md defaulted to exactly this; it is rejected).

## Design Principles

1. **Practise what you regulate.** The site itself should demonstrate integrity: accurate, current, nothing hidden behind sliders or vague claims.
2. **The register is the hero utility.** Checking a licence is the most valuable public act on the site; it gets prime placement, not a nav sub-item.
3. **Authority through restraint.** Confidence comes from precise typography, space and real information, never from decoration or superlatives.
4. **One door per audience.** Every visitor type finds their route within one screen, and the routes are named in their language, not the Commission's org chart.
5. **Rooted in Alderney.** The island is a genuine differentiator; use place with intent (light, coast, geography), not as generic stock scenery.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor across the site (public body obligation).
- Full keyboard operation, visible focus, skip link, logical heading order.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected: motion reduces to opacity only.
- Colour never the only signal (licence status, alerts, links).
- Mobile-first for the player journeys (register check, complaints), 16px minimum body text.
- Plain-English labels; legal terms explained on first use where practical.
